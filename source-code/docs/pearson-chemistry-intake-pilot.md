# Pearson Chemistry 4CH1 intake: preliminary local pilot

Date: 2026-10-05; updated on 2026-10-17. Scope: June 2025 standard `1C` and `2C` on the local machine only; no AKURU upload or release. `1CR` and `2CR` were inspected during preliminary inventory but are deferred beyond this pilot.

The catalogue in `scripts/catalog-pearson-chemistry.py` found 48 question-paper sets across 14 sessions: 48 question papers, 48 mark schemes, and 46 examiner reports. The missing reports are June 2021 `1C` and `2C`. Three January 2020 filenames say `PaperCR`, but their PDFs identify Paper `2CR`. The June 2019 `1C` question paper and June 2024 `1C` examiner report need manual code verification because a code was not recoverable from their first four pages.

For a preliminary standard/regional comparison, June 2025 `1C` and `1CR` each have a 28-page, fully text-extractable question PDF. Their exact companion schemes and reports are also text-extractable. The `1C` scheme is 16 pages and report 93 pages; the `1CR` scheme is 15 pages and report 118 pages. Each question PDF contains 27 embedded images; each report contains many embedded images. This makes image and shared-stem review a release requirement even though every page has text. The first-page text in each question PDF identifies the intended component; the companion title pages identify `1C` and `1CR` respectively.

The read-only pilot script reconciles **10 questions / 110 marks** on `1C` and **11 questions / 110 marks** on `1CR` with the totals in their exact mark schemes and the declared paper totals. The source page 2 of both question PDFs is a landscape periodic table, not a question. `1CR` Question 1 concerns states of matter and matches the currently available Chemistry Topic 1; that does not make the *whole* `1CR` paper eligible for a Topic 1-only Student. The ignored source folder contains `june-2025-pilot-manifest.json` with question-level page/mark pointers and explicit unresolved review status.

The standard `2C` has **6 questions / 70 marks**. Running the pilot script with its default scope now creates a manifest for `1C` and `2C` only: **16 top-level question drafts, 87 visible subpart mark allocations and 6 companion PDFs**. Their internal codes and ordered question totals match their own schemes. The previous four-component inventory remains available in the worksheet, and the script can include regional variants only with `--include-regional`. Paper 2 is not restricted to the textbook units currently uploaded: the Pearson specification says it may assess any topic area, including the additional bold “C” content. This affects Student eligibility and mock construction, not whether the source can be reviewed now.

Representative question inspection confirms why the import cannot simply publish extracted text. `1C` Question 1 (8 marks) depends on an atom diagram and has three subparts; the scheme awards 3, 4 and 1 marks with positional and alternative-answer notes. `1CR` Question 1 (6 marks) asks about state changes and gas particles; its scheme gives alternative accepted terms and awards any three relevant particle properties. Examiner-report commentary for these questions is separate evidence, not a replacement for either scheme. The pilot must preserve the figure and subpart relationships before Student release.

This is a document-level and question-inventory preflight, **not** a validated question bank. The worksheet is in `docs/pearson-chemistry-june-2025-question-review.md`; its regional sections are deferred. The local manifest preserves raw question/scheme text for review. Before import, review individual question/subpart boundaries, correct marks, linked diagrams, answer schemes, examiner commentary, exact topic mappings and Student coverage. The local AKURU database currently has zero textbook, topic and document records, so a realistic local import needs a separately prepared Chemistry structure. The specification PDF is available locally for review but no interpreted mock-generation rules have been approved yet. Original past-paper sessions and grounded preparation advice must not be claimed ready from this preflight alone.

Initial visual spot check of `1C` Question 1 and `2C` Question 1 confirmed that extracted text alone omits essential layout: `1C` needs its atom diagram and answer table, while `2C` needs its shared substance-choice box across page 4 and the continuation on page 5. Their scheme pages contain tables with accepted alternatives and notes (including answer order and ignored terms). Numeric reconciliation is therefore insufficient for marking approval. Candidate examiner-report page numbers in the manifest are search hits, not approved links; for example, `2C` page 3 is a general introduction. Full review of marking entries and relevant report passages remains open.

The next pass rendered all 16 question page ranges and their corresponding scheme entries. Six marking-scheme entries span two PDF pages: `1C` Q5, Q6 and Q9; `2C` Q2, Q3 and Q5. The manifest and worksheet now record each full page range. The question-layout inventory is in the worksheet. Full transcription and approval of wording, marking alternatives and examiner commentary remain open. Candidate report pages now require an actual question heading, which excludes the generic `2C` introduction on page 3.

The six pilot PDFs (28 + 16 + 93 pages for `1C`; 20 + 12 + 76 pages for `2C`) rendered without errors locally. Their SHA-256 hashes in the refreshed manifest match the intake catalogue. The local PostgreSQL database currently has **0 textbooks, 0 topics and 0 documents**; thus a real Student paper attempt and topic-filtered mock cannot yet be run against genuine published local Chemistry coverage. This is an environment prerequisite, not a passing pilot gate. No production changes were made.

AKURU now requires an approved marking-scheme entry for the selected question from the exact source paper version before starting a Student assessment. The optional examiner report does not block an otherwise grounded assessment. Companion uploads inherit missing identity fields from the selected paper and reject conflicting session, component, variant or year values.
